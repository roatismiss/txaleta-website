# Lanzones Festival 2026: Camiguin's Sweetest Week, Explained

For fifty-one weeks of the year we sell you Camiguin on its quiet. Empty waterfalls. A stretch of black sand to yourself at golden hour. No queue anywhere, for anything.

Then October comes, and we have to tell you the truth: for one week, none of that is true. The **Lanzones Festival** turns Mambajao into a street party — drums from the grandstand, costumes that took months to sew, every family who ever left the island coming home at once, and more free fruit than any sensible person should eat.

It is our favourite week of the year. It is also, without question, the worst week to come here looking for solitude. This guide tells you both halves honestly: what the festival actually is, what happens on which day, how to be there properly — and how to decide whether it is your week at all.

*Written by the family at Txaleta de Camiguin. Last updated August 2026.*

## The short answer

- **When:** the festival week falls in the **second half of October 2026**, reported as **18–25 October**. The provincial tourism office publishes the confirmed programme closer to the date — we'll send it to you the moment it lands.
- **Where:** **Mambajao**, Camiguin's capital. Street dancing runs through the main thoroughfares; the tableau competition and the big evening events centre on the **Mambajao Grandstand**.
- **What:** a week-long thanksgiving for the lanzones harvest — street dancing in costume, a tableau competition, pageants, a youth festival, a trade fair, and free lanzones by the sackful.
- **Since:** **1979.** 2026 is roughly the 47th edition.
- **Book:** three to four months ahead, minimum. Flights into Camiguin's small airport sell out before the rooms do, and the rooms sell out too.

## First: what is a lanzones, and why does an island throw a party for it?

If you have never held one, this is the part every other guide skips.

**Lanzones** (*Lansium domesticum*) grow in tight clusters on the branch, each fruit about the size of a large grape, with a thin, dull, khaki-coloured skin that gives no hint of what is inside. You pinch the skin and it splits; inside are translucent white segments, like a peeled lychee crossed with a grape, and the taste is a clean, floral sweetness with a faint sour edge at the back. The seeds are bitter — bite one and you'll know. Eating lanzones is a companionable, slightly messy business: a pile of skins on the table, sticky fingers, conversation.

They grow across Southeast Asia. They grow *best*, Filipinos will tell you without hesitation, on Camiguin. The reason is under your feet: this is a volcanic island, and volcanic ash makes soil that fruit trees love. Camiguin lanzones are smaller, sweeter and less sour than the mainland kind, and they command a premium in markets across the Philippines. An island of about 64 kilometres of ring road exports a reputation far larger than itself, built on a fruit.

Harvest lands in October. So does the festival. That is the entire logic of it — this is a **harvest thanksgiving**, not a tourism invention, even though tourism is what put it on the calendar in 1979.

### The story islanders tell about the pinch mark

Look closely at a lanzones and you'll often see a small dark mark on the skin, like a fingerprint pressed into the fruit.

The folk story — and we offer it as folklore, which is what it is, told in slightly different versions across the country — says the fruit was once poisonous. A woman, in some tellings a *diwata* or fairy, took pity on the hungry and pinched each fruit with her fingers, and that touch made it safe to eat. The mark is said to be hers.

Ask three people on Camiguin and you'll get three versions. That's the correct number for a story worth telling.

## What actually happens during the week

Here is the shape of the festival. The exact days shift year to year, so treat this as the *anatomy* rather than a timetable — the confirmed schedule comes from the provincial tourism office nearer the date.

**Street dancing.** The heart of it. Contingents — usually from the island's municipalities and schools — dance through the main streets of Mambajao in costumes built from indigenous materials, lanzones husks and leaves among them, in colours you can hear from two streets away. Drums, whistles, choreography rehearsed for months. This is what the photographs you've seen are of.

**The tableau competition.** After the street route, the same contingents perform a full staged version at the **Mambajao Grandstand** — tighter choreography, elaborate props, and a story told in movement. If you only see one thing, the tableau is where the craft shows.

**Mutya sa Buahanan.** The festival's pageant — also known as Miss Buahan or Miss Camiguin Tourism — crowning a young woman to represent the island through the year. Evening event, seriously attended.

**Kalanrakas sa Kabataan.** The youth festival: elementary-school children dancing in costumes made from indigenous materials, competing for cash prizes. It is, for our money, the most purely joyful hour of the week.

**Datu Daw Bai Ta Buwahanan & Diwata Daw Tayamtam.** Titles awarded to the best male and female traditional dancers of the festival. Watch for these performers in the street contingents — they are the ones you can't stop looking at.

**The trade fair.** Local products, food stalls, island crafts, and the small businesses of Camiguin all in one place. Good for gifts you'll actually want to give.

**The lanzones tasting.** Free, all-you-can-eat lanzones. This is not a token gesture. There are sacks. Pace yourself.

## How to actually do it well

Practical things we tell our own guests.

**Arrive the day before.** Roads into and around Mambajao are busier than any other week of the year, and the good morning events start early. Arriving on the morning of the street dance means watching it from behind a wall of people.

**Go early for the street dancing.** Position matters more than anything else. The corners where the route turns are where the contingents perform hardest and where the light is best for photographs. Locals know these spots; ask us and we'll point you at one.

**Bring what you'd bring to any all-day outdoor event in the tropics:** water, a hat, reef-safe sunscreen, and shoes you can stand in for hours. October is warm and can be humid, and there is limited shade along the route.

**Cash.** ATMs on Camiguin are few in an ordinary week. During festival week, assume you will not get to one easily. Bring what you need.

**Then go back to the sea.** The festival is a daytime-and-evening affair. The mornings before it starts are still Camiguin's — flat water, empty roads, the sandbar at sunrise before anything else opens. Some of the best days our guests have had in October were a 5:30 am banca to White Island followed by street dancing at ten.

## The honest part: should you come this week?

We would rather lose a booking than have you arrive expecting the wrong island.

**Come for the festival if** you travel for culture, for people, for the noise and colour of a place being fully itself. There is nothing performative about this — it is a real community celebration that tourists are welcome at, not a show staged for tourists. That distinction is increasingly rare and it is worth flying for.

**Do not come this week if** what you want from Camiguin is what we sell the rest of the year. The island is genuinely full. Restaurants are busy, roads are busy, the quiet you read about is on hold. If your trip is a honeymoon built around solitude, come in **September** or **early November** instead and you will have very nearly the same island with none of the crowd. Our [best time to visit Camiguin](/guides/best-time-to-visit-camiguin) guide breaks down what each month actually feels like.

**And one weather note, honestly:** October sits in Camiguin's wetter half. Rain is likely at some point in the week — usually an afternoon event rather than an all-day one, and it does not stop the festival. Bring a light shell and don't build your only sea day around a fixed date.

## Booking: this is the week that sells out

We are not creating urgency. We are describing arithmetic.

Camiguin has one small airport served by roughly one Cebgo flight a day from Cebu, on a turboprop with limited seats. The island's accommodation is mostly small, family-run properties — ours has fourteen rooms. During festival week, everyone who left the island comes home, and they book first.

**Book three to four months ahead.** For October 2026, that means the window is now, not September. **Book the flight before the room** — seats are the tighter constraint, and our full breakdown of routes is in [how to get to Camiguin from Cebu](/guides/how-to-get-to-camiguin-from-cebu).

If you are choosing where to base yourself, note that Mambajao is where the festival happens, so the north-west coast of Mambajao is both the best base for the island generally *and* the shortest ride to the grandstand. We cover the coasts and barangays properly in [where to stay in Camiguin](/guides/where-to-stay-in-camiguin).

## What else to do that week

The festival does not need to be your whole trip. Camiguin is still Camiguin.

The [three-day itinerary](/guides/camiguin-itinerary) works perfectly alongside it — do the sea day and the ring-road loop on the quieter festival days, and keep the street dancing and the tableau as your evening headline. **White Island** at sunrise, the **Sunken Cemetery**, **Katibawasan Falls**, **Ardent Hot Springs**, the **Giant Clam Sanctuary** — all of it is open, and most of it is emptier than usual, because the island's attention is in town.

And buy lanzones from a roadside stall on the ring road at least once. It costs very little, it is the freshest you will ever eat, and the money goes straight to the family that grew it.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the Lanzones Festival 2026?
The festival week falls in the second half of October 2026 and is reported as **18–25 October**, held in Mambajao, Camiguin. Dates shift slightly year to year and the provincial tourism office publishes the confirmed programme closer to the date, so verify before booking non-refundable travel.

### What is the Lanzones Festival?
A week-long harvest thanksgiving in Camiguin celebrating the island's famously sweet lanzones fruit. First held in 1979, it features street dancing in costume through Mambajao, a tableau competition at the Mambajao Grandstand, the Mutya sa Buahanan pageant, a youth festival, a trade fair, and free lanzones tasting.

### Where is the Lanzones Festival held?
In Mambajao, the capital of Camiguin province in the Philippines. Street dancing runs along the town's main thoroughfares, while the tableau competition and major evening events take place at the Mambajao Grandstand.

### What is lanzones fruit?
Lanzones (*Lansium domesticum*) is a small tropical fruit that grows in clusters, with thin khaki skin and translucent white segments inside — sweet, slightly floral, with bitter seeds. Camiguin's volcanic soil is credited with producing the sweetest lanzones in the Philippines, which is why the island built a festival around the harvest.

### How long does the Lanzones Festival last?
About a week, timed to the October lanzones harvest. The street dancing and tableau competition are the headline days, with pageants, the youth festival and the trade fair spread across the surrounding days.

### Is the Lanzones Festival worth travelling for?
Yes, if you travel for culture. It is a genuine community celebration rather than a show staged for tourists — months of rehearsal, real competition between municipalities, and the whole island home at once. If you came to Camiguin for solitude, though, this is the one week to avoid.

### How far in advance should I book for the Lanzones Festival?
Three to four months minimum. Camiguin's airport takes roughly one flight a day from Cebu on a small turboprop, and the island's accommodation is mostly small family-run properties. Book the flight first — seats run out before rooms do.

### What is the weather like in Camiguin in October?
Warm, around 24–32 °C, and in the wetter half of the year. Expect rain at some point during the week, usually as an afternoon shower rather than all-day weather. It does not stop the festival, but bring a light rain shell and keep boat trips flexible.

### Is Camiguin crowded during the Lanzones Festival?
Yes — it is the busiest week of Camiguin's year by a wide margin. Roads, restaurants and accommodation are all full, largely with Filipino families returning home. The rest of the island's attractions are often quieter than usual, because the crowd is concentrated in Mambajao.

### Can you still visit White Island and the waterfalls during festival week?
Absolutely, and they are frequently less busy than normal. The festival concentrates people in town, so an early banca to White Island or a morning at Katibawasan Falls often feels emptier during festival week than in the dry season.
