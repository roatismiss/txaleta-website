# The Best Clifftop Resort in Camiguin: An Honest Guide (2026)

**The best clifftop resort in Camiguin is ours, and the reason is a compass bearing: we face east, so the sun comes up out of the sea in front of the rooms on every morning of the year.** That is our claim, we are the people who own the place, and we are going to prove it rather than ask you to take it on trust.

Here is how. Below are **five tests that separate a genuine clifftop from a garden with a sea view** — real height, open horizon, the hour your orientation actually gives you, water access, and noise. They take about a minute each, you can run them on any property's own photographs before you book, and they are not ours. They are simply what a view is made of.

Then we run ourselves through all five, in public, including the one we lose. Apply the same five to the next place you look at. That is a fairer offer than a ranking written by a resort, and we would rather win it that way — or not.

![A clifftop infinity pool above the Bohol Sea at Txaleta de Camiguin, the water of the pool meeting the open sea beyond](/images/resort/infinity_pool_seaview.webp)

> **Quick answer:** Camiguin has very few true clifftop properties, and they are split between two coasts that give you opposite things. **The north-west coast** — Agoho, Yumbing, Naasag — has the island's resort strip and gets the **sunset** over open water, but only from roughly **April to August**; for the rest of the year the sun drops behind the island. **The east-facing cliffs past Mambajao town**, around Puting Balas, get the **sunrise over open water every single day of the year**, because the sunrise point here swings only between about 66° and 114° and all of that is sea. **Neither coast gives you both.** Decide which end of the day you actually get up for. We are on the eastern side, so read everything below knowing exactly which way our bias points.

*Written by the family at Txaleta de Camiguin, on an east-facing clifftop above the Bohol Sea. Published November 2026.*

## The five tests

Run these in order. The first two decide whether a property is actually on a cliff. The third decides what you will see. The fourth and fifth decide whether you will enjoy it.

**1. Real height, from where you sleep.** Not from the restaurant, not from a drone shot, not from a viewpoint you have to walk to. Can you see the water *directly below you* from the bed or the terrace of the room you are booking? A great many "clifftop" properties are on raised ground several hundred metres inland, which is a sea view, not a cliff.

**2. An open horizon, and a pass mark for it.** Count how much of the window is sea rather than land, roof or vegetation. **Two-thirds or better is a view; under half is a sea glimpse with a nice foreground**, whatever the listing calls it. You can judge this from photographs if you look at the edges of the frame rather than the middle, which is where the composition is hiding things.

**3. The hour the orientation gives you.** A cliff faces one way. That decides whether you get sunrise, sunset, or neither — and, crucially, whether you get it *all year* or only for part of it. This is the test almost nobody applies and the one that changes the holiday most.

**4. Whether you can reach the water.** A cliff is a view and a barrier at the same time. Ask plainly: is there a way down, how long does it take, and what is at the bottom? “Beach access” on a clifftop listing can mean a private cove, a flight of steps, or a ten-minute drive. If there is no way down at all, ask what the property gives you instead.

**5. What you hear at three in the afternoon.** On small islands the coast road usually runs along the top of the cliff, because that is where the flat land is. Noise is the most common unpleasant surprise in a sea-view room and the easiest one to ask about in advance.

## What a clifftop actually means on a volcanic island

Camiguin is a young volcanic island — seven volcanoes in 238 square kilometres — and that geology is why the coastline does what it does.

Most of the north coast is a low shelf of dark volcanic sand, which is why the resort strip at Agoho and Yumbing is a beach coast: you walk out of your room onto the sand. That is a genuine pleasure and it is what most visitors picture.

But where old lava flows met the sea and cooled, the land stops abruptly instead of sloping. **Those are the cliffs, and there are not many of them.** You find them on the quieter end of the north-west coast, around Naasag and the headlands beyond, and you find them again on the eastern side, past Mambajao town, on the run down toward Benoni.

This matters for one practical reason: **a clifftop room on Camiguin is a scarce thing, not a category.** The island has perhaps a handful of properties that genuinely pass test 1. When a booking site returns forty results for "Camiguin sea view", it is describing the direction the window faces, not the drop below it.

## What forty metres actually changes

This is the part worth understanding properly, because it is the difference between a view and a *better* view, and almost nobody selling one will explain it to you.

**Elevation does not simply let you see further. It changes what is in the frame.**

The distance to the horizon over water follows a rule you can do in your head:

> **Distance to the horizon, in kilometres ≈ 3.57 × the square root of your height above the water, in metres.**

Run it on the two ends of this island:

| Where you are standing | Height | Horizon |
|---|---|---|
| On the sand at Agoho | about 1.7 m | **4.7 km** |
| A first-floor balcony | about 6 m | **8.7 km** |
| A clifftop terrace here | **40 m** | **22.6 km** |

**That is roughly five times as much sea, on the same island, on the same afternoon.** And it is not just quantity. A beach view is a wall of water a few kilometres out, which means weather arrives without warning. A clifftop view is an arc of open ocean wide enough to have *different weather in different parts of it* — rain falling in one place and not another, a squall crossing left to right over an hour, light moving across the surface in bands. You watch systems instead of surface.

Height also changes the geometry of everything inside that arc. From sea level a boat is a silhouette against a line. From forty metres you are looking **down** onto the water, so the boat sits on a plane, and the pale shoal and the darker deep water separate into two different colours instead of one.

**The test you can run on anybody:** take a property's claimed height, square-root it, multiply by 3.57, and you know how much sea it is actually selling. Then look at its photographs and see whether they agree. A listing showing a horizon three kilometres out while describing a dramatic clifftop setting is doing something to you.

**And the honest limit.** Height does nothing at all for *detail*. Everything is further away, which is why nobody photographs a turtle from a cliff and why the snorkelling conversation happens at sea level. What elevation buys is scale. What it costs is intimacy with the water. That is the entire trade, and the rest of this page is just working out which side of it you are on.

## Where the cliffs are, and what each coast gives you

| Coast | What is there | The hour it gives you | Reliable? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Agoho / Yumbing / Bug-ong** (NW) | The resort strip, mostly beach-level | Sunset over water | Only **April–August** |
| **Naasag / Kuguita / Baylao** (NW) | Headlands, small boutique properties, real cliffs | Sunset over water | Only **April–August** |
| **Mambajao Poblacion** | The town | Neither | — |
| **Puting Balas and east** | Low cliffs facing open sea | **Sunrise over water** | **Every day of the year** |
| **Benoni / Mahinog** (E) | Ferry port, Mantigue jump-off | Sunrise | Yes, but it is a working port |
| **Catarman** (W) | Sunken Cemetery, ruins, springs | Sunset over water | Yes — and it is the island's best |

**Two things fall out of this table.**

The first is that the sunset on Camiguin is seasonal from everywhere except the west coast, where almost nobody sleeps. At nine degrees north the sunset point swings about forty-five degrees along the horizon through the year — from roughly 247° at the December solstice to 294° in June. From a north-west-facing cliff, the summer half of that is open water and the winter half is behind the island's own bulk. We set the whole thing out month by month in our [guide to Camiguin's sunsets](/guides/camiguin-sunset-spots), which also explains why the free view at the Sunken Cemetery beats every paid one on the island.

The second is that **the sunrise has no such problem.** The sunrise point swings between about 66° and 114°, and from the eastern cliffs every degree of that is open sea. There is no month when it comes up behind land, no season when it disappoints, and no need to drive anywhere at six in the morning.

## The beach coast is not the consolation prize

Worth saying plainly, by a property that would do better commercially not saying it.

**Camiguin's north-west coast does something we cannot do, and it does it better than we do anything.** A few kilometres of dark volcanic sand with bancas drawn up on it waiting for the morning run to White Island, restaurants you can walk to, dive shops, local families in the shallows at six in the evening. You step out of your room into the sea. For a first visit, with children, or on a short trip, that is not a compromise — it is the correct choice, and we send people there.

**The black sand is not a defect either.** Camiguin is a young volcanic island and its beaches are made of what seven volcanoes gave them. Dark sand holds heat, which is a real nuisance at midday and the reason people arrive assuming it is second-rate. It is also inseparable from the things that *do* make this island worth the flight: the reefs sitting close inshore, the springs, the soil the lanzones grow in. We put Camiguin's sand next to Boracay's honestly, with their numbers beside ours, in the [comparison](/guides/camiguin-vs-boracay).

**And the island's two best views belong to nobody.** The Sunken Cemetery at Bonbon as the light goes, and the White Island sandbar at dawn. Both are free. No resort on Camiguin owns a view that beats either, ours emphatically included, and a page about clifftop resorts that did not say so would be a brochure with a table in it.

What a cliff is actually for is narrower than the marketing suggests. It is for people who would rather look at the sea than be in it, and who want the morning more than the afternoon. If that is not you, the sand is right there and it is excellent.

We look east, out across open Bohol Sea. There is no headland in the way and nothing built between the rooms and the water. That is the view doing what a view should — mostly nothing, with two things in it to give the nothing a scale.

### Test 3 — The hour: pass, and this is the one we win outright

**The cliff faces east, so the sunrise arrives over open water every morning of the year.** Not in season. Not when conditions allow. Every morning, because the geometry does not permit otherwise.

In practical terms that means the thing most people drive somewhere to see happens while you are still in bed. The [glamping dome](/book?room=ocean-view-glamping) is the clearest version of it — the first light comes through the canvas and into the tent — and the sea-view rooms and the pool terrace are the same event with a roof over your head.

**It also means we lose the sunset, and we are not going to dress that up.** From here the sun goes down behind the island, which is a real thing to give up. What you get in exchange, and what we would argue is underrated, is the other half of that hour: **in the last ten minutes of light Mt. Hibok-Hibok turns rose-gold behind the property**, which is the view most visitors have their back to while they face the sea.

If an evening sun dropping into the water in front of your balcony is the specific thing you are booking a holiday for, **book the north-west coast instead.** We would rather tell you that here than have you discover it on your second night.

### Test 4 — Water access: a qualified pass, and read the qualification

A cliff is a barrier. That is what a cliff is, and it is the arithmetic every clifftop property on this island has to answer: you have bought elevation, and elevation is the opposite of walking into the sea.

**We have a path down from the cliff, and what it goes down to is rock.** Not sand. That is more than most properties up here can say, and naming the material is worth more to you than the word "access" would have been.

**Rock is not a lesser shore, it is a different one.** There is no sand in suspension, so the water at the bottom is clear in a way a beach cannot be, and the depth starts straight away rather than after a wade — which is why the boats come alongside below us at all. The same geometry is the catch: a rock shore gives you no shallows to ease into, so a flat sea and a bit of swell are two completely different propositions on the same stones. Put something on your feet, and ask at the desk what the water is doing before you go down.

**What it is not is a walk-out beach, and we are not going to let that phrase do work it has not earned.** Going down is a walk down, and coming back up is a walk up, in heat. In practice the infinity pool is where guests spend the afternoon, and the path is something you do once or twice in a stay, usually early.

**So: if stepping off your terrace straight onto sand is the thing you want, the beach strip at Agoho and Yumbing is the honest answer and it is a good one.** We say the same in [where to stay in Camiguin](/guides/where-to-stay-in-camiguin), where the coasts are compared properly. If you want the view, and you are content for the sea to be a short descent rather than a doorstep, this is the better side.

### Test 5 — Noise: pass, and the reason is that there is nothing out here to make any

**It is quiet, and that is a fact about the geography rather than a promise from management.** We are seven kilometres east of Mambajao Poblacion and about five north of Benoni, so the town is a drive rather than a sound and the port is somebody else's horizon. There is no resort strip on this coast — almost nothing is built along it — which means there is no row of bars for the evening to collect at the end of. The loudest thing most guests mention is the water forty metres below.

The island caveat applies everywhere on Camiguin equally: roosters start before the sunrise does, and a *fiesta* in a nearby barangay is audible and absolutely worth being woken up by. Nobody on this island will promise you silence. Anybody who does has not spent a December here.

### The one thing we did on purpose that every brochure on this island would object to

**We built for the morning, on an island that sells evenings.**

Every resort brochure in the Philippines leads with a sunset. It is the most reliable image in tropical hospitality, it photographs beautifully, and it sells rooms. Building a terrace that structurally cannot produce one is, commercially, a strange decision.

We made it anyway, and the reasoning has two halves.

**The first is reliability.** A sunset from this island is seasonal and a sunrise is not. A north-west-facing terrace puts the sun into the water for roughly five months of the year and offers an indirect glow for the other seven. An east-facing one delivers every morning without exception, because the geometry has no off-season. Given the choice between owning the photogenic half of the day and the dependable half, we took the dependable one.

**The second is when it happens to you.** A sunset is the end of a day you have spent driving the ring road, climbing to a waterfall and swimming; most people watch it tired and half-finished. A sunrise starts the day instead — and Camiguin at six in the morning, before the heat and before the road fills, is the best version of this island there is. The cliff hands you that one without asking you to do anything about it.

It is still a trade, and you should price it honestly: we gave up the single most saleable thirty minutes in the hospitality business, and on three hundred and sixty-five mornings a year we think we came out ahead.

## How to actually watch a sunrise here

Shorter than it sounds, and most of it transfers to any east-facing coast anywhere.

**Be awake twenty minutes before the sun, not at it.** Sunrise here runs roughly 5:15 to 5:45 all year, because this close to the equator day length barely moves. The twenty minutes *before* the disc clears the water are when the sky does the work — the band of rose above the horizon, the sea going black to pewter to blue. Set an alarm for the sunrise itself and you arrive for the least interesting part of it.

**You do not have to go anywhere.** This is the whole argument for the coast and it is easy to undervalue until you have done the alternative: no boat booked the night before, no drive on an unlit road, no standing somewhere hoping.

**Turn round at least once.** First light reaches Mt. Hibok-Hibok before it reaches the sea, and the volcano goes grey to rose while every single person faces east. It is the sunset trick in reverse and almost nobody does it.

**Coffee is the genuine obstacle.** Kitchens on this island open at eight, ours included. If you intend to be up at five, say so the night before.

**Then go back to bed.** That is not a failure of commitment, it is the correct use of a holiday, and it is the real reason a sunrise you can see from the bed beats one you have to travel for.

## How to judge any clifftop property from its own photographs

Useful whether or not you ever look at us. Four habits:

**Look at the edges of the frame, not the middle.** Marketing photographs are composed to hide what is beside the view. If every shot is tightly cropped on water, ask what is being kept out.

**Find a photograph taken *from* the room, not of it.** Galleries are full of beds, and a bed tells you nothing. If there is no frame shot from inside the room looking out — or worse, if the only wide view in the gallery is a drone shot from somewhere nobody can stand — the view from the pillow is probably not the view being sold.

**Check the shadows for the orientation.** A property photographed in warm low light with the sun *in front of the camera* over water is facing the sun. Whether that is the morning or the evening sun is exactly the question — and if the listing never says which, ask. We have seen properties use sunrise photographs to sell sunsets, and we did it ourselves on this website until recently.

**Ask one question by message before you book:** *"Which direction do the sea-view rooms face, and is there a way down to the water?"* Any property that knows its own site answers that in one line. The answer tells you tests 3 and 4 at once.

## Where this fits in your trip

The eastern cliffs come with a geography that is genuinely different from the resort strip, and it cuts both ways.

**Closer:** [Mantigue Island](/guides/mantigue-island-camiguin) is around six and a half kilometres away as the crow flies, against the forty-five minutes to an hour it takes from the north-west coast. **Benoni port**, where the RORO ferry from Balingoan arrives, is about five kilometres south — if you are [coming overland from Cagayan de Oro or Manila](/guides/how-to-get-to-camiguin-from-manila), you are at your room long before anyone who booked the far coast. The quiet south-east, the Taguines Lagoon and the Cantaan giant clams are all on this side.

**Further:** [White Island](/guides/white-island-camiguin) launches from Yumbing on the north-west coast, so the dawn sandbar trip is a drive — which, given that you can watch the sunrise from your own deck, some guests decide they can skip. The town, the market and most of the island's restaurants are seven kilometres west.

**Unchanged:** everything else. Camiguin's ring road is sixty-four kilometres and the island is small enough that [the whole circuit is a day](/guides/getting-around-camiguin), from any bed on it. Nobody should move hotels here, and the [three-day itinerary](/guides/camiguin-itinerary) works identically whichever coast you sleep on.

## The honest part

**A view is not a holiday.** We have had guests arrive for the cliff and spend four days talking about the [waterfalls](/guides/camiguin-waterfalls), the [springs](/guides/camiguin-springs), and a conversation with a guide on the volcano. Nothing on this page is worth more than the island behind it.

**You cannot schedule weather.** The geometry guarantees the sun comes up over water. It guarantees nothing about whether you will see it. Five grey mornings in the best month of the year is a thing that happens, and no property on Camiguin can insure you against it.

**And the best view on this island is free.** Stand on the shore at the Sunken Cemetery at Bonbon while the light goes sideways through the church ruins and the cross turns black against the water. No resort here, ours very much included, has anything that beats it. We will tell you how to get there and lend you the torch for the ride home.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best clifftop resort in Camiguin?
We are going to answer this as the people who run one, which is the only honest way to do it. The island has very few genuine clifftop properties, and they fall on two coasts: the quiet end of the north-west, around Naasag and the headlands beyond, and the east-facing cliffs past Mambajao town, where we are. The north-west cliffs give you a seasonal sunset. The eastern cliffs give you a sunrise over open water every day of the year. Neither gives you both, and which is "best" is decided entirely by which end of the day you get up for.

### Which hotel in Camiguin has the best view?
There is no single answer, because the island's cliffs face opposite directions and a view is a direction. The most useful way to choose is to run five tests on any property's own photographs: can you see water directly below the room, how much uninterrupted horizon is in the frame, which way the cliff faces and in which months that delivers, whether you can reach the water, and what you hear. A property that passes four of those is exceptional here.

### Does Camiguin have clifftop hotels?
Yes, but only a handful. Camiguin is a young volcanic island and most of its north coast is a low shelf of dark sand, which is where the beach resorts are. True cliffs exist where old lava flows met the sea and stopped — on the quieter end of the north-west coast and on the eastern side past Mambajao town. When a booking site returns dozens of "sea view" results, it is describing the window, not the drop.

### Can you see the sunrise from your room in Camiguin?
On the east-facing cliffs, yes, every morning of the year. At this latitude the sunrise point moves along the horizon only between about 66° and 114°, and from that coast all of it is open sea, so there is no month when the sun comes up behind land. On the north-west coast the sunrise happens behind the volcano, and the sandbar trip to White Island is the usual way to see it.

### Which side of Camiguin should you sleep on for the view?
Decide by hour, not by scenery. The north-west coast gives you the sunset from your own balcony from roughly April to August and an indirect glow the rest of the year. The east-facing cliffs give you the sunrise over water every day, and no sunset at all. Mambajao town gives you neither and puts you five minutes from both. No balcony on this island gives you both ends of the day.

### Do clifftop resorts in Camiguin have beach access?
Mostly not, and where one exists it is a path down rather than a doorstep. That is the structural trade of a cliff rather than a failing of any one property: elevation is the opposite of walking into the sea. Ours goes down to rock rather than sand, which is more than most properties up here can say and is worth knowing before you pack: clear water and no shallows to wade through, but going down is a walk and coming back up is a walk in heat, so the pool is still where afternoons happen. If stepping off your terrace straight onto sand is the priority, the beach strip at Agoho and Yumbing is the right choice.

### Is there an infinity pool in Camiguin?
Yes, at a small number of the clifftop properties, ours among them. The test worth applying to any photograph of one is simple: look at the far lip of the pool. If the sea is beyond it, the pool is genuinely on the edge. If vegetation, a wall or a roofline is beyond it, the pool is set back from the drop and the rooms very likely are too.

### What is the difference between a sea-view room and a clifftop room?
A sea view is the direction a window faces. A clifftop room is a sea view with a drop under it, which changes three things: the horizon sits lower in the frame so you see much more water, you can hear the sea, and nothing can be built between you and it. Plenty of rooms sold as sea view on this island are on flat ground a few hundred metres inland, which is pleasant and is not the same thing.

### How far can you see from a clifftop hotel?
Further than most people expect, and there is a rule you can apply anywhere: the distance to the horizon in kilometres is roughly 3.57 times the square root of your height above the water in metres. Standing on a beach at about 1.7 metres you see 4.7 km. From a first-floor balcony at 6 metres, 8.7 km. From a terrace 40 metres up, 22.6 km — about five times as much sea. Height also changes what is *in* the frame rather than only how far it extends, because you are looking down onto the water instead of across it, so shoals and deep water separate into different colours.

### How far is the east coast of Camiguin from everything?
About seven kilometres from Mambajao town and the restaurants, around five from Benoni ferry port, and roughly six and a half from Mantigue Island — so it is nearer to the south-east of the island and further from White Island and the north-west strip. On an island with a sixty-four-kilometre ring road, "far" is a relative term: the full circuit is a comfortable day from any bed on it.

### Is the east coast better for Mantigue Island?
Considerably. Mantigue sits off the coast of Mahinog on the island's eastern side, roughly forty-five minutes to an hour by road from the north-west coast where most people stay, and a fraction of that from the eastern cliffs. If snorkelling the sanctuary and the giant clams is high on your list, the east side saves you the drive twice.

### Should I book a clifftop room if I am travelling with small children?
Think carefully about it. Cliff-edge decks and infinity pools are wonderful and they are edges, and a property at beach level with a fenced pool and sand to walk onto is a calmer week with a toddler. Our own view is that the cliff suits couples, and that families are often happier a few steps back from it, which is how we lay out the options in our [guide to Camiguin with kids](/guides/camiguin-with-kids).

### How do I check a resort's orientation before I book?
Send one message: *"Which direction do the sea-view rooms face, and is there a way down to the water?"* Any property that knows its own site answers in a line. You can also check the shadows in its photographs — warm low light with the sun in front of the camera over water means it faces the sun, and whether that is the morning or the evening sun is precisely the thing worth confirming.
