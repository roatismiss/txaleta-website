# Where to Stay in Camiguin: Best Areas & How to Choose (2026)

Search **where to stay in Camiguin** and you get a list. Twenty properties, ranked by a number, with no explanation of why the top one is forty minutes from everything you flew here to see.

The list is the wrong tool. Camiguin is a small island — a ring road of about sixty-four kilometres, an hour to drive without stopping — and yet *where on that ring you sleep* changes your holiday more than any amenity on any booking page. One coast has the airport, the restaurants, the boats to White Island and almost every resort on the island. The rest has almost nothing, on purpose.

So we're going to do this by **area**, not by star rating. We'll tell you what each stretch of coast is actually like, who it suits, what it costs, and what to check before you press book. Then, because it would be strange to pretend otherwise, we'll tell you plainly where we fit — we're a fourteen-room family resort on a clifftop in Mambajao, and we're not going to rank our neighbours. We'll just make sure you can choose properly.

*Written by the family at Txaleta de Camiguin. Last updated August 2026.*

## The short answer

**Stay on the north-west coast of Mambajao.** Almost everyone should. It's where the airport is, where the boats to White Island leave from, where the restaurants and the hot springs are, and where the island's real accommodation sits — a ribbon of coast through the barangays of **Kuguita, Baylao, Naasag, Yumbing, Agoho and Bug-ong**, all within about fifteen minutes of each other.

Three refinements:

- **First time on the island, three or four nights?** North-west Mambajao, near the White Island jump-off. Don't overthink it.
- **Want a view rather than beach access?** The **clifftop stretch** of the same coast, where the land rises above the water and the sunsets arrive uninterrupted.
- **Coming for the ferry, for Mantigue, or for a working farmstay?** Look at **Benoni / Mahinog** on the east coast — but know what you're trading away.

Everything else is detail. Useful detail, but detail.

## Camiguin's areas at a glance

| Area | What it is | Best for | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Agoho / Yumbing / Bug-ong** (NW Mambajao) | The resort strip: beach front, restaurants, dive shops, White Island boats | First-timers, families, anyone without a car | Busiest stretch on the island — which here still means quiet |
| **Naasag / Kuguita / Baylao** (NW Mambajao) | The quieter end of the same coast; cliffs, headlands, small resorts | Couples, views, sunsets, calm | Fewer walk-out restaurants; you'll ride or drive to dinner |
| **Mambajao Poblacion** (town) | The capital: market, ATMs, bakeries, pharmacies, hospital | Budget stays, long trips, practicalities | Not on the beach; it's a town, not a view |
| **Catarman** (west) | Sunken Cemetery, church ruins, cold springs, Tuasan Falls | Sightseeing-heavy trips, cheap local stays | Thin on good accommodation and dining |
| **Benoni / Mahinog** (east) | The RORO ferry port and the jump-off for Mantigue Island | Ferry arrivals, divers, a night before an early sailing | Far from the airport, the boats to White Island and the restaurants |
| **Guinsiliban / Sagay** (south) | The quietest quarter, the Giant Clam Sanctuary at Cantaan | Total seclusion, homestays, repeat visitors | Very little infrastructure; you need your own wheels |

## Why the north-west coast wins

It isn't an accident that the resorts cluster on one stretch of one municipality. Four things happen to sit there together.

**The airport is there.** Camiguin has its own runway at Mambajao, with near-daily Cebgo flights from Cebu in around fifty-five minutes. From the terminal, the north-west coast is a ten-to-twenty-minute drive. Stay here and your first afternoon is a swim, not a transfer. (The routes and timings are all in our guide to [getting to Camiguin from Cebu](/guides/how-to-get-to-camiguin-from-cebu).)

**The boats leave from here.** **White Island** — the bare white sandbar that put Camiguin on Instagram — is a ten-to-fifteen-minute banca ride from the beaches at Yumbing and Agoho. Sleeping on this coast is the difference between a 5:30 am sunrise crossing and a 4:45 am alarm and a drive in the dark.

**Ardent Hot Springs is up the hill behind it**, along with the road to the Old Volcano walkway and the trailhead for guided ascents of **Mt. Hibok-Hibok**.

**And the food is here.** Camiguin is not a restaurant island. Outside Mambajao and this coast, dinner options thin out fast and close early.

The rest of the ring — Catarman's ruins and cold springs, Mahinog's Mantigue Island, Guinsiliban's giant clams — is genuinely worth your days. It just isn't where you want your bed. On an island this small, everything is a day trip from the north-west coast. Very little is a day trip *to* it.

## Area by area

### Agoho, Yumbing and Bug-ong — the resort strip

If Camiguin has a tourist centre, this is it, and it's about as unthreatening as tourist centres come: a few kilometres of coast with beach resorts, a handful of restaurants and cafés, dive operators, scooter rental, and the bancas drawn up on the sand waiting for the morning run to White Island.

Sand here is a mix — dark volcanic grey in most places, paler in a few — because Camiguin is a volcanic island and its beaches are honest about it. If you're expecting Boracay, read our [Camiguin vs Siquijor comparison](/guides/camiguin-vs-siquijor) first; we settle the black-sand question there properly.

**Stay here if:** it's your first visit, you're travelling with children, you want to walk to dinner, or you want a boat outside your door.

**Don't if:** you're chasing solitude and long views. This is the one stretch where you'll see other travellers.

### Naasag, Kuguita, Baylao — the quiet end

Keep going and the coast changes character. The land lifts, headlands push out into the Bohol Sea, and the properties get smaller and further apart. Some sit on the sand; some sit thirty feet above it, which is a different proposition entirely — the sea at eye level from your bed, the sunset arriving over open water with nothing in front of it.

This is where the island's boutique end has grown, and it's where we are: a clifftop in **Puting Balas**, about fifteen minutes from the runway.

**Stay here if:** you're a couple, you're on honeymoon, you value the view and the quiet over walking distance to a beach bar, or you simply sleep better without a road outside.

**Don't if:** you want to step out of your room onto a stretch of public beach with a rental kayak on it.

### Mambajao Poblacion — the town

The capital is small, tidy and useful: the public market, the ATMs (few, and occasionally out of cash — bring some with you), the pharmacies, the hospital, the bakeries, the government office where the tourism people are genuinely helpful. **Katibawasan Falls** is a short ride inland.

Accommodation here is inns and guesthouses rather than resorts, and the price reflects it.

**Stay here if:** you're on a tight budget, staying a week or more, or you like being able to buy things.

**Don't if:** the point of the trip is the sea. It's ten minutes away, but it isn't outside.

### Catarman — the sightseeing coast

West and south of Mambajao, Catarman holds a remarkable density of the island's famous places: the **Sunken Cemetery** with its great cross standing out in the water, the roofless **Gui-ob Church Ruins**, the spring-fed **Sto. Niño Cold Spring**, **Tuasan Falls**, and the Stations-of-the-Cross **Walkway to the Old Volcano**.

What it doesn't hold is much in the way of places to stay or eat. There are small local lodgings and homestays, and they can be lovely and very cheap.

**Stay here if:** you're travelling on a budget, you have your own transport, and you want to be first at the Sunken Cemetery in the morning light.

**Don't if:** you want dinner options, or a pool, or a hot shower you can rely on.

### Benoni and Mahinog — the ferry and Mantigue side

Benoni is Camiguin's main port for the RORO ferries to Balingoan on the mainland, and Mahinog is the jump-off for **Mantigue Island**, a small forested island with white sand, a marine sanctuary and good snorkelling. There's a lagoon at Benoni, a few resorts, and a genuinely different rhythm — this is a working coast.

The catch is geography. You're roughly forty-five minutes to an hour from the airport and the White Island boats, on the opposite side of the ring.

**Stay here if:** you're arriving or leaving by ferry, you're diving this side, or you want Mantigue at sunrise.

**Don't if:** it's a short trip. You'll spend it driving.

### Guinsiliban and Sagay — the quiet south

The least-visited quarter of an already quiet island: coastal villages, the **Cantaan Giant Clam Sanctuary** with its shallows carpeted in giant clams, and not much else by design. Homestays and small local resorts.

**Stay here if:** you've been to Camiguin before, you have a vehicle, and you want to disappear.

**Don't if:** it's your first visit and you have three days.

## What accommodation in Camiguin actually costs

Camiguin is not an expensive island, and the spread is wide. As a rough guide to what you'll see on the booking sites — always check live rates, since these move with the season:

| Type | Typical nightly rate | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Hostel dorm / basic room | ₱800 – ₱1,500 | Fan or basic aircon, shared or simple bath, no frills |
| Guesthouse / inn | ₱1,500 – ₱2,800 | Private room, aircon, hot water, often no pool |
| Mid-range beach resort | ₱2,800 – ₱5,000 | Pool, restaurant, beach access, tours arranged |
| Boutique / premium | ₱5,000 – ₱12,000+ | Sea views, design, breakfast, real service |
| Glamping / unusual stays | Varies widely | A proper bed somewhere you'd never otherwise sleep |

Prices climb in the four windows that sell out every year: **Holy Week**, the **Filipino summer** from late March to May, **Lanzones Festival week** in October, and **Christmas to New Year**. Our [best time to visit Camiguin](/guides/best-time-to-visit-camiguin) guide covers exactly when those fall and how far ahead to book.

A full worked budget for the whole trip — flights, boats, entrance fees and all — is in [how much a Camiguin trip costs](/guides/camiguin-travel-cost).

Two costs travellers forget: **airport or port transfer** (ask whether it's included — ours is), and **the ring road itself**. A scooter runs around ₱450 a day, an SUV around ₱2,850, and a driver on top of that if you'd rather look out of the window than at the road.

## What to check before you book — the Camiguin-specific list

Standard hotel checklists don't cover island realities. These do.

1. **How far from the airport, honestly?** Ask for minutes, not kilometres. The ring road is slow and beautiful in equal measure.
2. **How far from the White Island jump-off?** If the answer isn't "we launch from here" or "ten minutes", your sunrise trip becomes a pre-dawn drive.
3. **Is there back-up power?** Island-wide outages happen. Ask whether there's a generator and what it runs.
4. **Hot water and water pressure.** Not universal outside the resorts, and worth confirming in writing.
5. **Is breakfast included, and where is it served?** On this island, the answer "on the terrace, over the sea" is worth more than the food.
6. **Wi-Fi speed, if you're working.** Ask for a number, not a yes.
7. **Do they arrange the boat, the trek, the driver?** A resort that books your banca, your Hibok-Hibok guide and your airport pickup saves you a day of WhatsApp.
8. **Beach access vs sea view.** These are different products. Decide which you're buying before you look at photos.
9. **Sand colour.** If white sand under your feet is a non-negotiable, know that Camiguin's is mostly volcanic and the white stuff is a short boat ride offshore.
10. **Card or cash?** ATMs are few. Confirm you can pay how you intend to.

## How many nights, and where to put them

The mistake we watch people make is three nights split across two areas, chasing efficiency. Don't. The island is small enough that one good base covers all of it.

- **3 nights** — one base, north-west Mambajao. Sea day, ring-road day, slow day. Our [3-day Camiguin itinerary](/guides/camiguin-itinerary) lays it out hour by hour.
- **5 nights** — same base. Add Mantigue, add the volcano, add an afternoon of doing nothing.
- **7+ nights** — same base *still*, and consider a night at Benoni or in the south at the end if you want a change of air before the ferry.

If you're combining Camiguin with another island, the sequencing matters more than the hotel: we cover the routes and the honest travel times in [Camiguin vs Siquijor](/guides/camiguin-vs-siquijor) and in our roundup of the [best islands in the Philippines](/guides/best-islands-in-the-philippines).

## Book direct or book on an OTA?

Both work. Here's the honest trade-off.

**Booking sites** are excellent for comparing, for loyalty points, and for free cancellation if your plans are soft. Use them without guilt.

**Booking direct** — with us or with any small island property — usually gets you the better room in the same category, the flexibility to move a date without a fight, and a person who reads your message. It also means more of what you pay stays on the island rather than going to a commission. On a fourteen-room family resort, that difference is somebody's training, somebody's job.

If you're unsure: message the property first. How a small resort answers a question in August tells you exactly how it will look after you in November.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the best area to stay in Camiguin?
The north-west coast of Mambajao — the barangays of Kuguita, Baylao, Naasag, Yumbing, Agoho and Bug-ong. It has the airport, almost all of the island's resorts and restaurants, the boats to White Island, and quick access to Ardent Hot Springs and Mt. Hibok-Hibok. Everywhere else on the ring road is better as a day trip than as a base.

### Should I stay in Agoho or Yumbing?
They're neighbours, minutes apart, and both sit near the White Island jump-off. Agoho and Yumbing are the livelier end with more resorts, restaurants and dive shops — the easy choice for first-timers and families. The quieter barangays just along the coast (Naasag, Kuguita, Baylao) suit couples and anyone who wants a view and silence over walking distance to dinner.

### Where should I stay to visit White Island?
Anywhere on the north-west coast of Mambajao. The bancas leave from the beaches at Yumbing and Agoho and the crossing takes ten to fifteen minutes. Go at sunrise — the sandbar has no shade at all, and both the light and the tide are better early.

### How much does accommodation in Camiguin cost?
Roughly ₱800–₱1,500 for a hostel or basic room, ₱1,500–₱2,800 for a guesthouse, ₱2,800–₱5,000 for a mid-range beach resort, and ₱5,000 and up for boutique and premium stays. Rates rise sharply during Holy Week, the Filipino summer, Lanzones Festival week in October and the Christmas–New Year period.

### Is it better to stay near the airport or near the ferry port?
Near the airport, for almost everyone. Camiguin's airport at Mambajao sits on the same north-west coast as the resorts, the restaurants and the White Island boats. Benoni port is on the opposite side of the island, about 45 minutes to an hour away — worth a night only if you're sailing early or heading to Mantigue Island.

### Does Camiguin have luxury or boutique resorts?
Yes, though on a small scale — this is an island of family-run properties rather than international chains. The boutique end is concentrated on the north-west coast of Mambajao and includes clifftop resorts with infinity pools, sea-view rooms and glamping. Expect design and personal service rather than large-resort facilities.

### Where should couples or honeymooners stay in Camiguin?
On the quieter, clifftop end of the north-west Mambajao coast, where the land rises above the sea and the sunsets come in over open water. Look for sea-view rooms, private terraces or cliff-edge glamping, and a property small enough to arrange your days for you.

### Is Camiguin good for families?
Very. It's safe, quiet, short on traffic, and everything is close together. Stay on the north-west coast, choose a property with a pool as well as sea access, and pick family rooms over connecting doubles. The springs, the sandbar and the giant clam sanctuary all work brilliantly with children.

### How many nights should I book in Camiguin?
Three nights covers the highlights, five lets you slow down, and seven is when people start talking about coming back. Whatever the length, keep one base on the north-west coast rather than moving hotels — the island is small enough that everything is a day trip from there.

### Do I need to rent a scooter or car if I stay in Mambajao?
Not necessarily. Many resorts arrange a driver, and boats, tours and treks are usually booked through your hosts. But the 64-kilometre ring road is one of the island's great pleasures, and a scooter (around ₱450 a day) or an SUV (around ₱2,850) gives you the whole of it at your own pace.

### Is it cheaper to book direct or through a booking site?
The rate is often similar, but booking direct with a small island property tends to get you a better room in the same category, more flexibility with dates, and a real person handling your requests — plus more of what you pay stays with the local business rather than going to commission.

### When do Camiguin hotels sell out?
Four windows: Holy Week (late March or early April), the Filipino summer from late March to May, Lanzones Festival week in the second half of October, and roughly 22 December to 3 January. Book those three to four months ahead. Three to six weeks is comfortable the rest of the year.
