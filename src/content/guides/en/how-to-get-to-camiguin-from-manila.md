# How to Get to Camiguin from Manila (2026): Every Route

Let's get the disappointing part out of the way in the first line, because every other guide buries it under four hundred words of preamble: **there is no direct flight from Manila to Camiguin, and there has never been one.**

Camiguin's runway takes one small turboprop a day. It comes from Cebu. That is the entire airline network of this island.

So the real question is not "how do I fly from Manila to Camiguin" — it is **which connection you make, and which one still gets you here today.** There are two that work, one that works if the journey is the point, and one that people attempt out of stubbornness and regret somewhere outside Butuan at four in the morning.

We are the family who meets the arrivals. We have watched guests come in on all four. Here is the honest map.

![Mt. Hibok-Hibok rising over the Bohol Sea, seen from the water off Camiguin's north coast](/images/experiences/hero_group_boat.webp)

> **Quick answer:** How to get to Camiguin from Manila — there is no direct flight. The fastest route is **Manila → Cebu → Camiguin**, two flights, about 5–6 hours door-to-door including the layover. The most *reliable* route is **Manila → Cagayan de Oro → bus to Balingoan → ferry to Benoni**, about 7–8 hours but on flights that run many times a day. Budget roughly ₱4,000–6,500 either way, depending on how early you book.

The pattern: **fly to Cebu if the connection lines up, fly to Cagayan de Oro if it does not.** Now the detail that decides which.

## Route 1: Manila → Cebu → Camiguin (the fast one)

This is the route we would book first, and it is the only one that puts you on the island without touching a boat.

**Leg one: Manila (MNL) to Cebu (CEB).** One of the busiest domestic routes in the country. Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines and AirAsia all fly it many times a day, the flight is about **1 hour 25 minutes**, and fares swing enormously — roughly **₱1,500–4,500** one way depending on how far ahead you book and whether a seat sale is running.

**Leg two: Cebu (CEB) to Camiguin (CGM).** The 55-minute Cebgo turboprop, roughly **one flight a day**, fares typically **₱1,300–1,900**. You book it through Cebu Pacific, but the aircraft on this run is Cebgo — an ATR. There is no other airline flying nonstop into Camiguin. We have written that leg up in full in [our guide to reaching Camiguin from Cebu](/guides/how-to-get-to-camiguin-from-cebu), including what the little airport is like when you land.

### The connection trap nobody warns you about

Here is the part that turns this route from clean to painful, and it is the single most useful paragraph on this page.

**The Camiguin flight has often departed Cebu early.** Departures have been reported leaving around 06:20 and landing about 07:15. If that is the schedule on your dates, then **no Manila flight the same morning can connect to it.** You would need to leave Manila at an hour that does not exist on the timetable.

Which means, in practice, one of two things:

- **Your dates fall on a day with a later Camiguin departure.** Frequency has been reported anywhere between about nine and sixteen flights a week, so afternoon slots do appear. Check the actual timetable for your dates before you commit to this route.
- **Or you overnight in Cebu** and take the early flight fresh the next morning. This is no hardship — Mactan has hotels five minutes from the terminal — but it turns a one-day journey into a two-day one, and it is the reason a lot of Manila travellers end up on Route 2 instead.

Two more things about this connection:

**It is almost certainly a self-transfer.** Two separate tickets, even inside the same airline group, usually means collecting your bags, re-checking them, and absorbing the delay yourself if leg one runs late. Leave **at least three hours** in Cebu. Not two. The Camiguin flight is one a day; missing it costs you a night.

**The Camiguin leg sells out.** It is a small turboprop with limited seat allocation on a route with no alternative. Around the [Lanzones Festival in October](/guides/camiguin-lanzones-festival) and over the December holidays it goes early. Book the CEB–CGM leg *first*, then build the Manila flight around it — not the other way round.

### Step by step

1. **Fly MNL → CEB.** About 1 hr 25 min. Take a morning departure if you are connecting the same day.
2. **Transfer at Mactan-Cebu.** Allow three hours minimum. Domestic to domestic, but collect and re-check bags if the tickets are separate.
3. **Fly CEB → CGM on Cebgo.** About 55 minutes over the Camotes and the Bohol Sea. One of the better approaches in the Philippines.
4. **Camiguin Airport to Mambajao.** About 7 km. A tricycle runs from around ₱50. There is **no Grab on Camiguin**, so if you are staying with us, [tell us your flight and we will be at the gate](/book).

**Total:** roughly 5–6 hours door-to-door on a same-day connection, and about **₱4,000–6,000** all in.

## Route 2: Manila → Cagayan de Oro → Benoni (the reliable one)

This is the route we point most Manila guests to, and it is worth understanding why the *longer* journey is often the *better* one.

The Camiguin flight is a single daily thread. Cagayan de Oro's **Laguindingan airport (CGY)** is a proper regional hub with many flights a day from Manila on Cebu Pacific, PAL and AirAsia. You trade two hours of your afternoon for a route that does not collapse when one aircraft goes tech.

**Leg one: Manila (MNL) to Cagayan de Oro (CGY).** About **1 hour 35 minutes**, several departures daily, fares broadly in the same **₱1,500–4,500** band as the Cebu route. Book the morning flight — the reason is the ferry, and we will get to it.

**Leg two: Laguindingan to Balingoan port.** This is the leg that catches people out, so read it twice.

**Laguindingan airport is west of Cagayan de Oro. Balingoan port is east of it.** The airport sits roughly 45 km on one side of the city, the port roughly 90 km on the other. Almost nobody tells you this, and it changes how you plan the leg:

- **The cheap way** is a shuttle or bus into CDO (Agora or Bulua terminal), then a **Rural Transit bus toward Balingoan** — about **2–2.5 hours** and **₱160–200**, running frequently through the day. Cheap, easy, and you will be on it beside people carrying boxes of bread.
- **The fast way** is a chartered van straight from the airport to Balingoan, bypassing the city. It costs a great deal more and saves an hour or so, and it earns its price only if you landed late and the last ferry is the thing standing between you and a bed.

**Leg three: the RoRo ferry, Balingoan → Benoni.** Multiple sailings a day. **Super Shuttle Ferry** runs about **1 hr 30 min for around ₱359**; **St. Benedict Ocean Shipping** is closer to **1 hour at about ₱300**. Board half an hour early. Sailings thin out in the evening — which is why you take the morning flight from Manila. If the sea has been rough, they thin out further.

**Leg four: Benoni to Mambajao.** A shared multicab or jeepney is about **₱25**; a private multicab, up to eight people, runs around **₱1,700**. It is a short cross-island hop along the ring road, not an expedition. Tell us which boat you are on and we will help you sort the last leg.

**Total:** roughly **7–8 hours** door-to-door and **₱4,200–6,500**. Longer than Route 1 on paper. Far more likely to actually happen on the day you planned it.

## So which one should you book?

The honest decision rule, the way we would give it to a friend:

**Take Route 1 (via Cebu) if** the CEB–CGM flight has a seat on your date, the timing allows a real connection with three hours of slack, and you would rather not deal with buses and boats after a flight. It is genuinely the nicer arrival.

**Take Route 2 (via Cagayan de Oro) if** the Camiguin flight is full, expensive, or scheduled at an hour that forces a Cebu overnight — or if you are travelling in the last week of October or over Christmas, when that one aircraft is the tightest bottleneck on the island. Take it too if you simply hate connection anxiety. The bus and the ferry both run all day; nothing on this route is a single point of failure.

**A quiet third option:** fly into Cagayan de Oro on the way in and out through Camiguin's own airport on the way home, or the reverse. You see more, you are not held hostage to one flight in both directions, and open-jaw domestic tickets in the Philippines usually cost about the same as two one-ways anyway.

## Cagayan de Oro to Camiguin, on its own

If you are already in Mindanao — living in CDO, working in Cagayan de Oro, coming off a Bukidnon trip — the whole question collapses to one line: **Agora or Bulua terminal → Rural Transit bus to Balingoan → RoRo to Benoni → multicab to Mambajao.** Around **four hours** and under **₱600** if you stay on public transport the whole way.

It is a genuinely pleasant half-day. The highway runs along the Macajalar Bay coast, the crossing is short, and the volcano gets bigger the whole way over. Plenty of people from CDO do this as a long weekend, which is exactly what it is good for — see our [three-day Camiguin itinerary](/guides/camiguin-itinerary) for how to spend it.

## The two ways to arrive by sea, and the one to avoid

**Via Bohol.** If you are already island-hopping, the chain runs Cebu → Tagbilaran fastcraft (about 2 hr, around ₱965), bus to Jagna (about 2 hr, from around ₱150), then the Jagna → **Balbagon** ferry — roughly 4.5 hours for about ₱965, sailing **once daily in the early afternoon**. From Manila this is realistically a two-day journey, because you will not land at Mactan and make that single afternoon departure. It is lovely and it is slow. Do it if the journey is the point.

**Overland from Manila.** Yes, it is technically possible to reach Camiguin by bus and ferry from Manila — down through the Bicol peninsula, across to Samar and Leyte, over into Mindanao, then east. It takes two to three days, it is not meaningfully cheaper than a promo fare, and we have never once met a guest who arrived that way and recommended it. If a seat sale exists, fly.

## Benoni or Balbagon? Get this right

Camiguin has two ports, and confusing them is the most common arrival mistake we see:

- **Benoni** — the main port, on the southeast side. This is where the **Balingoan RoRo** lands. Route 2 arrives here.
- **Balbagon** — the smaller port near Mambajao. This is where the **Jagna, Bohol ferry** lands.

Both feed into Mambajao, where the island's life and our gate sit. But when a booking app or a bus conductor asks which port, the answer depends entirely on the route you took. Tell us your arrival point and we will meet you at the right one.

## The honest part

Camiguin is not hard to reach. It is *awkwardly* connected, which is a different thing, and the difference matters when you are planning.

Nothing about this journey is difficult. The flights are short, the bus is comfortable, the ferry is a boat with plastic chairs and a good view. What it demands is **slack in your schedule.** One daily flight and an evening-thinning ferry timetable mean the route punishes a tight plan and shrugs at a loose one. Give yourself a spare half-day at the front of the trip and Camiguin is easy. Try to land at six in the evening and drive straight to a dinner reservation, and the island will teach you something.

The other honest thing: this awkwardness is why the island is still the way it is. There is no ride-hailing here, no chain hotel, no strip. Every extra connection between Manila and this cliff is part of why you will have the sandbar mostly to yourself at sunrise. We are not going to pretend that is a coincidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a direct flight from Manila to Camiguin?
No. There is no nonstop flight between Manila and Camiguin, and there never has been. Camiguin Airport takes roughly one turboprop a day, operated by Cebgo from Cebu. From Manila you connect through Cebu, or you fly to Cagayan de Oro and finish the journey by bus and ferry.

### How long does it take to get from Manila to Camiguin?
About five to six hours door-to-door via Cebu if the connection lines up on the same day, and about seven to eight hours via Cagayan de Oro. If the Cebu connection does not work on your dates, budget for an overnight in Cebu, which makes it a two-day journey.

### What is the cheapest way to get to Camiguin from Manila?
A promo fare to Cagayan de Oro plus public transport the rest of the way — bus to Balingoan at ₱160–200, ferry to Benoni at ₱300–359, multicab to Mambajao at about ₱25. The airfare dominates the total, so the cheapest trip is really the one booked earliest, not the one with the fewest flights.

### Should I fly to Cebu or Cagayan de Oro for Camiguin?
Cebu is faster when the single daily Camiguin flight has a seat and the timing allows a real connection. Cagayan de Oro is more reliable, because it has many flights a day and the onward bus and ferry both run frequently. In peak season — late October and the December holidays — Cagayan de Oro is usually the safer booking.

### How do I get from Laguindingan airport to Camiguin?
Take a shuttle or bus into Cagayan de Oro's Agora or Bulua terminal, then a Rural Transit bus toward Balingoan port — about two to two and a half hours. From Balingoan, the RoRo ferry crosses to Benoni on Camiguin in one to one and a half hours. Note that the airport is west of the city and the port is east of it, so you pass through or around CDO either way.

### How much is the ferry from Balingoan to Camiguin?
Around ₱300–359 depending on the operator. Super Shuttle Ferry takes about an hour and a half for roughly ₱359; St. Benedict Ocean Shipping is closer to an hour at about ₱300. There are multiple sailings a day, but they thin out in the evening, so aim to be at Balingoan by mid-afternoon.

### Can I get to Camiguin from Manila in one day?
Usually yes, if you take the first flight out. Via Cagayan de Oro a morning departure from Manila comfortably makes an afternoon ferry. Via Cebu it depends entirely on whether the Camiguin flight departs late enough that day — often it does not, which is what forces the overnight.

### How far in advance should I book the Cebu to Camiguin flight?
As far ahead as you can, and always before you book the Manila leg. It is one small turboprop a day with limited seat allocation and no alternative airline. It sells out around the Lanzones Festival in October and over the December holidays.

### Which port does the ferry from Cagayan de Oro arrive at?
Benoni, on Camiguin's southeast side. The other port, Balbagon near Mambajao, serves the ferry from Jagna in Bohol. Getting these two confused is the most common arrival mistake, so check which one your route ends at before you arrange a pick-up.

### Is there Grab or a taxi app on Camiguin?
No. There is no ride-hailing on the island at all. Your options from the airport or the port are a tricycle, a shared or private multicab, or a transfer arranged by your accommodation — which is why we build the pick-up into the stay rather than leaving you to negotiate it after a travel day.

### Can I travel from Manila to Camiguin entirely by land and sea?
Technically yes, via the Bicol peninsula, Samar, Leyte and northern Mindanao, but it takes two to three days and costs about the same as a flight booked in advance. Unless the overland journey is the point of the trip, fly at least as far as Cebu or Cagayan de Oro.

### Is Camiguin worth the extra connection?
We are biased, so take this as declared bias: yes, and the awkward connection is part of the reason. One flight a day and a ferry timetable are what have kept the island free of chain hotels, tour-bus strips and crowds on the sandbar at sunrise. The journey is the filter.
