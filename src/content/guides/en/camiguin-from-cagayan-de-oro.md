# Camiguin from Cagayan de Oro: The Laguindingan–Balingoan Route (2026)

If you have landed at Laguindingan, or you are already living or working in Cagayan de Oro, Camiguin is closer than almost anyone tells you — and the one thing every generic guide gets wrong is treating the airport and the port as if they were next to each other. They are not. **Laguindingan airport sits west of the city. Balingoan port, where the ferry to Camiguin leaves from, sits east of it.** Getting that backwards is the single most common planning mistake we see from guests coming this way.

We are the family who meets the boat at Benoni. This is the route broken down properly: which terminal to use, which bus, which ferry company, and the one decision — morning flight or afternoon — that decides whether you sleep in Camiguin tonight or in Cagayan de Oro.

![A passenger ferry crossing open water toward Camiguin, the route taken from Balingoan port to Benoni](/images/experiences/hero_group_boat.webp)

> **Quick answer:** From Laguindingan airport, get into Cagayan de Oro (shuttle or taxi to Bulua or Agora terminal), take a **Rural Transit bus toward Balingoan** (about 2–2.5 hours, ₱160–200), then the **RoRo ferry to Benoni** (1–1.5 hours, ₱300–359). Total from touchdown: roughly **4–5 hours** and under **₱700**. If you are already in CDO rather than flying in, skip the airport leg and the whole trip is closer to **four hours door-to-door**. Take a **morning flight or an early bus** — sailings thin out by evening, and missing the last ferry means a night in Balingoan or back in the city.

*Written by the family at Txaleta de Camiguin, on the island this route ends at. Published November 2026.*

## Why this route confuses people who have never done it

Two facts, and almost nothing else, govern this entire journey:

**Laguindingan (CGY) is about 45 km west of Cagayan de Oro.** It opened in 2013 to replace the old city-centre airport, and it is a proper regional hub — Cebu Pacific, Philippine Airlines and AirAsia all serve it, with several flights a day from Manila and connections from elsewhere in the country. Check live availability for your dates; frequency from cities other than Manila varies.

**Balingoan port is about 90 km east of the city, on the opposite side.** This is where the RoRo ferries to Camiguin's Benoni port depart. The airport and the port are, in effect, on either end of Cagayan de Oro — which means however you arrive, the city itself sits in the middle of your route, not beside it.

That single geography fact is why this page exists on its own rather than as a footnote on another one: the decisions that matter — which terminal, which bus, whether to pay for a van — are specific enough to deserve their own answer.

## Route A: you are already in or near Cagayan de Oro

If you live in CDO, you are visiting from elsewhere in Mindanao, or you simply flew in the day before and are starting fresh, the route collapses to one clean line:

**Bulua or Agora terminal → Rural Transit bus toward Balingoan → RoRo ferry to Benoni → multicab to Mambajao.**

- **Terminal to Balingoan:** Rural Transit buses run frequently through the day, about **2–2.5 hours** for **₱160–200**. Bulua terminal, on the city's north side, is usually the more convenient of the two for this direction.
- **The ferry:** 1–1.5 hours, ₱300–359 depending on operator (details below).
- **Benoni to Mambajao:** a shared multicab runs about ₱25; a private one, up to eight people, around ₱1,700.

**Total: around four hours and under ₱600 if you stay on public transport the whole way.** It is a genuinely pleasant half-day — the highway runs along the Macajalar Bay coast, the crossing is short, and Mt. Hibok-Hibok gets bigger the whole way over. It is exactly the kind of trip CDO-based travellers do as a long weekend; our [three-day Camiguin itinerary](/guides/camiguin-itinerary) is built for arrivals like this.

## Route B: you are flying into Laguindingan

This is the leg that catches people out, because the airport does not sit on the way to the port — it sits on the opposite side of the city from it.

**The cheap way.** An airport shuttle or taxi into the city (Bulua or Agora terminal, roughly 45 km, 45–60 minutes depending on traffic), then the same Rural Transit bus to Balingoan described above. Add it up and you are looking at **3–3.5 hours and ₱250–350** from the runway to the port, before the ferry. This is what most backpackers and budget travellers do, and there is nothing wrong with it as long as your flight lands with daylight to spare.

**The fast way.** A chartered van direct from Laguindingan to Balingoan, bypassing the city centre entirely. It costs considerably more — arrange it through your accommodation or a local transfer operator rather than hoping one is waiting at arrivals — and it saves perhaps an hour over the bus option. It earns its price in exactly one situation: **you landed late, and the last ferry is the only thing standing between you and a bed on Camiguin tonight.**

### The decision that actually matters: what time do you land?

| You land at Laguindingan | What we'd do |
|---|---|
| **Morning (before 11am)** | Take the cheap route. You will reach Balingoan well before the sailings thin out, with time to eat on the way. |
| **Early afternoon** | Still fine on the bus if you move promptly from the terminal — do not linger over lunch in the city. |
| **Late afternoon or evening** | Pay for the van, or plan to overnight. Sailings get sparse after dark and can stop altogether in rough weather. CDO has no shortage of hotels near the airport and downtown if you need one. |

## The ferry crossing, Balingoan to Benoni

Multiple sailings run most days. The two main operators:

- **Super Shuttle Ferry** — about **1 hour 30 minutes**, roughly **₱359**.
- **St. Benedict Ocean Shipping** — closer to **1 hour**, around **₱300**.

Board at least half an hour before departure — queues at the pier move slower than they look, especially with cargo loading alongside passengers. Sailings are frequent through the morning and early afternoon and thin out noticeably by evening; if the sea has been rough that week, expect further gaps. Camiguin's two ports are easy to confuse: **Benoni**, on the southeast coast, is where this ferry lands. **Balbagon**, near Mambajao, is a different port entirely, served by the ferry from Jagna in Bohol — see [Camiguin vs Bohol](/guides/camiguin-vs-bohol) if you are weighing that crossing instead.

## Step by step

1. **Land at Laguindingan**, or start from wherever you are in Cagayan de Oro.
2. **Get to Bulua or Agora terminal** — shuttle, taxi, or your own transport, roughly 45 minutes from the airport.
3. **Board a Rural Transit bus toward Balingoan.** About 2–2.5 hours, ₱160–200.
4. **Buy your ferry ticket at Balingoan pier** and board at least 30 minutes early. 1–1.5 hours to Benoni, ₱300–359.
5. **From Benoni, take a multicab or jeepney to Mambajao** — shared for about ₱25, private for around ₱1,700 for up to eight people. Tell us which boat you are on and we will help sort the last leg.

## When this is the right route, and when it isn't

**Take this route if** you are based in or flying into Cagayan de Oro, Laguindingan has more flights on your dates than Camiguin's own airport, or the single daily Cebu–Camiguin turboprop is sold out or badly timed for a Cebu connection.

**Fly direct via Cebu instead if** your dates line up with the one daily Cebgo flight into Camiguin and you would rather not deal with a bus and a boat after travelling — see our [guide to reaching Camiguin from Cebu](/guides/how-to-get-to-camiguin-from-cebu) for that route, including what the connection looks like from Manila in our [Manila to Camiguin guide](/guides/how-to-get-to-camiguin-from-manila).

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is Laguindingan airport from Balingoan port?
Roughly 90 km apart with Cagayan de Oro city in between — the airport sits about 45 km west of the city, the port about 90 km east of it. There is no direct road that skips the city; every route to Balingoan passes through or around CDO.

### How long does it take from Laguindingan to Camiguin?
About 4–5 hours total if you arrive by mid-morning: roughly an hour to the bus terminal, 2–2.5 hours on the bus to Balingoan, and 1–1.5 hours on the ferry to Benoni. Add time for the multicab to Mambajao after that.

### Is there a faster way than the bus?
Yes — a chartered van direct from Laguindingan to Balingoan, bypassing the city. It costs considerably more than the bus-and-shuttle combination and saves about an hour. It is worth it mainly when you have landed late and need to catch the last ferry of the day.

### What if I miss the last ferry from Balingoan?
Cagayan de Oro has plenty of accommodation near the airport and downtown if you need to overnight. Balingoan itself has very limited options, so if there is any risk of missing the last sailing, it is better to stop in the city and try again the next morning.

### Which terminal should I use in Cagayan de Oro?
Bulua terminal, on the city's north side, is usually the more convenient starting point for buses heading toward Balingoan. Agora terminal also serves routes in that direction; either works, but confirm with your driver or shuttle which one they are headed to.

### How much does the whole trip cost from Laguindingan to Camiguin?
Budgeted on public transport: roughly ₱150–300 from the airport into the city, ₱160–200 for the bus to Balingoan, ₱300–359 for the ferry, and ₱25 for a shared multicab into Mambajao — under ₱900 all in, not counting the flight itself.

### Can I do this trip in one day from Manila?
Yes, if you take an early-morning Manila to Cagayan de Oro flight. It comfortably makes an afternoon ferry. For the full Manila-side comparison against the Cebu route, see our [Manila to Camiguin guide](/guides/how-to-get-to-camiguin-from-manila).

### Is this route better than flying directly into Camiguin from Cebu?
It depends on your dates. Camiguin's own airport takes one small turboprop a day from Cebu, which sells out around the Lanzones Festival and the December holidays. Laguindingan has many more flights and the Balingoan ferry runs several times daily, so this route is the more resilient booking when the direct flight is tight or unavailable.
