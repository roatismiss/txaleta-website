# Camiguin vs Siargao: Which Island Suits You (2026)

This comparison is easier than most, because these two islands are not really competing for the same person.

**Siargao is the social one.** Surf, cafés, hostels, a scene, people. It went from a secret to the Philippines' most talked-about island in about a decade, and it has the cafés and the crowds to prove it.

**Camiguin is the quiet one.** Volcanoes, springs, a sandbar, no nightlife whatsoever, and — genuinely — nobody in your photographs.

We live on Camiguin, so read this knowing exactly where we're standing. We'll be straight about what Siargao does better, because it does several things better, and then be equally straight about who should skip it.

*Written by the family at Txaleta de Camiguin. Last updated August 2026.*

## Camiguin vs Siargao at a glance

| | **Camiguin** | **Siargao** |
|---|---|---|
| **Character** | Volcanic, slow, romantic, genuinely quiet | Surf town, social, young, rapidly developed |
| **Best for** | Couples, families, slow travellers, springs and volcano | Surfers, solo travellers, digital nomads, nightlife |
| **Surf** | None to speak of | The Philippines' surfing capital — Cloud 9 and dozens of breaks |
| **Beaches** | Dark volcanic sand; white sand offshore at the sandbar and Mantigue | Palm-fringed coast, lagoons, white-sand offshore islets |
| **Signature sight** | White Island sandbar + the Sunken Cemetery | Cloud 9 boardwalk + Sugba Lagoon |
| **Nightlife** | Essentially none | Real bars, music, a scene |
| **Cafés / working** | Very limited | Excellent — a genuine nomad hub |
| **Crowds** | Very low | Moderate to high in season |
| **Getting there** | Own airport at Mambajao, ~1 flight/day from Cebu | Own airport (Sayak), more frequent flights from Cebu and Manila |
| **Days needed** | 3 for highlights, 5–7 to relax | 4–7, longer if you surf |
| **Cost** | Lower across the board | Higher — developed prices |

**Choose Siargao** if you surf, if you want a social scene, if you'll work from cafés, or if the trip is about meeting people.

**Choose Camiguin** if you want volcanoes, hot springs, an empty sandbar at sunrise, a genuinely uncrowded island, and no expectation to be sociable after dark.

## Credit where it's due: what Siargao does better

We won't be grudging about it.

**The surf, obviously.** Siargao is the Philippines' surfing capital, and Cloud 9 is a genuinely world-class reef break with a name that reaches well beyond the country. There are breaks for every level, boards to rent everywhere, and an entire culture built around it. Camiguin has nothing remotely comparable and never will — we're a sheltered volcanic island in the Bohol Sea, which is precisely why our water is calm.

**The cafés and the working infrastructure.** Siargao developed a proper café culture — good coffee, decent wifi, places to sit with a laptop for four hours. It's become a real digital-nomad base. Camiguin has not, and if you need to work reliably from a café, we'd honestly steer you elsewhere.

**The social side.** If you're travelling solo and want to meet people, Siargao does it effortlessly — hostels, group tours, bars, a scene that absorbs newcomers. Camiguin is a place for people who arrive with the company they want.

**The lagoons and island hopping.** Sugba Lagoon and the day trips out to the offshore islets are excellent, and the scenery is spectacular in a way that photographs extremely well.

**Flight frequency.** Siargao's Sayak airport takes more flights and from more places than Camiguin's single near-daily turboprop from Cebu. Getting there is simply easier to schedule.

If you read that list and thought "yes, that's my holiday" — go to Siargao. We mean it.

## Where Camiguin pulls ahead

### It's still actually quiet

This is the whole argument, and it's the one thing Siargao has lost.

Siargao's transformation over the past decade has been dramatic. That brought cafés, restaurants, flights and jobs — genuinely good things for the island — and it also brought people. In season, General Luna is busy, the popular spots have queues, and the version of Siargao that people fell in love with in 2015 is not the one you'll arrive at.

Camiguin hasn't been discovered in the same way. There's no beach strip. No party row. You can have a stretch of coast to yourself at golden hour without planning it. If **absence** is what you want from an island — of crowds, of noise, of needing to book a sunset spot — Camiguin is where that still exists.

### More distinct things to do per square kilometre

Camiguin is the **Island Born of Fire**, and it's stitched with more genuinely different experiences than an island you can drive around in an hour has any right to hold.

Volcano-warmed [hot springs](/guides/camiguin-springs) and a spring-fed cold pool and a naturally carbonated soda pool, all within a few kilometres. Two [waterfalls](/guides/camiguin-waterfalls). A [sunken cemetery](/guides/sunken-cemetery-camiguin) with a coral garden grown over it. A [white sandbar](/guides/white-island-camiguin) in open water. A [giant clam sanctuary](/guides/mantigue-island-camiguin). An [active volcano](/guides/hibok-hibok-hike) you can climb. And [three bird species](/guides/birdwatching-camiguin-hibok-hibok) that exist nowhere else on Earth.

Siargao's draw is concentrated: surf, lagoons, islets, scene. Excellent, but narrower. The [full list](/guides/things-to-do-in-camiguin) of what's here runs to twenty-five things.

### It costs less

Siargao's development brought developed prices. Camiguin remains genuinely cheap: entrance fees around ₱50–75, a scooter ₱450 a day, a carinderia meal ₱80–150, boat fares charged per boat rather than per head. Our [full cost breakdown](/guides/camiguin-travel-cost) has every figure.

### It's better for couples and families

Siargao is brilliant if you're 26 and travelling alone. Camiguin is better if you're on [honeymoon](/guides/camiguin-honeymoon), if you want a clifftop with nobody on it, or if you're travelling [with children](/guides/camiguin-with-kids) and want short distances, calm water and no bar noise at midnight.

## The honest case against Camiguin

Since we've been honest about Siargao, here's the reverse.

**No surf.** If you came to the Philippines to surf, Camiguin is the wrong island. Full stop.

**No nightlife.** A drink on a terrace and then bed. For some people that's a feature; for others it's a dealbreaker, and there's no point discovering which you are on day two.

**Black-sand main beaches.** Camiguin is volcanic and its ring-road beaches are dark. The white sand is offshore and spectacular but you take a boat to it. Siargao's coastline photographs better.

**A small restaurant scene.** Good places exist, but you will not have a different celebrated restaurant every night. Most people eat where they're staying.

**Fewer flights.** Roughly one small turboprop a day from Cebu, which sells out. Book early.

## Can you do both?

Yes, but be realistic about the geography — they're on opposite sides of Mindanao and there's no quick hop between them. Realistically you're routing through **Cebu**, which means a flight out and a flight back in, and losing the better part of a day to it.

**If you're doing both:** give Siargao four to seven days, then finish on Camiguin. Doing it the other way round means going from quiet to loud, which is the wrong direction for a holiday to end.

**If you only have ten days total**, we'd honestly pick one and do it properly rather than spending a day and a half in airports.

## Frequently asked questions

### Camiguin or Siargao — which is better?
Neither is better; they suit different travellers. Siargao wins for surfing, café culture, nightlife and meeting people. Camiguin wins for volcanic scenery, hot and cold springs, genuine quiet, lower prices and suitability for couples and families. Pick by what you actually want from the week.

### Is Siargao too crowded now?
It's considerably busier than it was a decade ago. Development brought cafés, restaurants and flights along with visitors, and in season General Luna and the popular spots are genuinely busy. It's still a wonderful island — just not a secret one.

### Is Camiguin a good alternative to Siargao?
For travellers who liked the idea of Siargao before it became popular, yes. Camiguin offers the uncrowded, unhurried island experience with volcanoes, springs and a sandbar — but no surf, no nightlife and a much smaller restaurant scene.

### Does Camiguin have surfing?
No. Camiguin is a sheltered volcanic island in the Bohol Sea with calm water — which is what makes its snorkelling, sandbar trips and swimming good, and what makes it useless for surfing. Siargao is the Philippines' surf destination.

### Which is cheaper, Camiguin or Siargao?
Camiguin, across the board. Entrance fees run around ₱50–75, a scooter is ₱450 a day, and local meals are ₱80–150. Siargao's rapid development brought developed prices for accommodation, food and activities.

### Which is better for couples, Camiguin or Siargao?
Camiguin, for most couples. It's quieter, has clifftop accommodation with sea views and cliff-edge glamping, and nothing about it demands you be sociable. Siargao suits couples who want a livelier trip with bars and a scene.

### Which is easier to get to?
Siargao. Its Sayak airport takes more flights from more places, including Cebu and Manila. Camiguin has its own airport at Mambajao but takes roughly one small turboprop a day from Cebu, which sells out — book early.

### Can you visit Camiguin and Siargao in one trip?
Yes, but they're on opposite sides of Mindanao with no quick connection, so you route through Cebu and lose most of a day. Give Siargao four to seven days and finish on Camiguin. With ten days or fewer, we'd pick one and do it properly.

### How many days do you need in Camiguin?
Three days covers the highlights, five lets you slow down, and seven is when people start planning a return. Siargao generally wants four to seven, and longer if you're surfing.

### Which island is quieter?
Camiguin, by a wide margin. It has no beach strip, no party row and very low visitor numbers even in the dry season. The one exception is Lanzones Festival week in late October, when the whole island fills up.

## Come home to Camiguin

If you want a board under your feet and a bar at the end of the day, Siargao is waiting and it's very good at that. We'll wave you off happily.

But if what you actually pictured was an empty sandbar at sunrise, a spring warmed by a volcano, and an evening where the loudest thing is the sea below the cliff — that's here, and it's still here, which is getting rarer every year.
