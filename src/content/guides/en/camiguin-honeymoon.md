# Camiguin Honeymoon: A Slow, Romantic Island Guide (2026)

Most honeymoon guides to the Philippines send you to the same three places, and all three have the same problem: everyone else is also on honeymoon there.

**Camiguin** is the quiet answer. A small volcanic island in the Bohol Sea with no party strip, no beach clubs, no queue for the sunset — and a set of things that happen to be perfect for two people who have just spent a year organising something for everyone else. Mornings that start on flat water. Afternoons in volcano-warmed springs. Evenings where the loudest thing is the sea below the cliff.

We host honeymooners most weeks of the year from our clifftop in Mambajao, and this is the guide we'd give you over the phone: what makes this island right for it, exactly how to spend five days, when to come, and the honest reasons it might not suit you at all.

*Written by the family at Txaleta de Camiguin. Last updated August 2026.*

## The short answer

- **Why here:** genuinely uncrowded, very safe, small enough that nothing is an expedition, and dramatic — seven volcanoes, a sandbar in the open sea, springs in the forest.
- **How many nights:** **five**. Three is enough to see the island; five is enough to stop checking the time.
- **When:** **May, June or September** — warm, calm, and the island nearly to yourselves. Avoid Lanzones Festival week in October unless you want the noise.
- **Where:** the quiet, clifftop end of Mambajao's north-west coast, in a sea-view room or cliff-edge glamping.
- **Budget:** around ₱6,000–10,000+ per person per day at the comfortable end, plus flights. The full breakdown is in [how much a Camiguin trip costs](/guides/camiguin-travel-cost).
- **The honest catch:** no nightlife, black-sand main beaches, and a small restaurant scene. If those are dealbreakers, read on before you book.

## Why Camiguin, and not the famous ones

We won't pretend the well-known islands are bad. They're famous for reasons. But honeymoons have a particular requirement — *privacy* — and fame is the enemy of it.

**Boracay** is beautiful and permanently busy. **Palawan** is spectacular and increasingly booked solid, with island-hopping boats that run in convoy. **Siargao** is wonderful if you surf and social if you don't want to be.

Camiguin gives you the thing those places used to have. You can have a stretch of coast to yourselves at golden hour. You can be the only boat at a sandbar at sunrise. Nobody is queuing for anything, because there is no queue.

It also compresses beautifully. The ring road is sixty-four kilometres — you can circle the whole island in an hour. That means no internal flights, no long transfers, no days lost to logistics. On a honeymoon, that's not a small thing: every hour you don't spend in a van is an hour you spend together.

And it is **safe** in a way that visitors notice and struggle to name — the kind of island where people leave helmets hanging on parked scooters. That absence of low-level vigilance is a real part of relaxing.

If you're weighing it against its closest rival, we make the full honest case in [Camiguin vs Siquijor](/guides/camiguin-vs-siquijor).

## Where to stay

This is the decision that shapes the whole trip, because on a honeymoon **where you wake up is most of the holiday**.

Stay on the **north-west coast of Mambajao** — that's where the airport, the boats and the restaurants are. Then choose your end of it:

- **The clifftop end** (Naasag, Kuguita, Baylao, Puting Balas) — the land rises above the water, the sunsets arrive over open sea with nothing in front of them, and the properties are small and far apart. This is the honeymoon end.
- **The beach end** (Agoho, Yumbing, Bug-ong) — livelier, more walkable, more options for dinner. Better if you want to stroll to a restaurant.

We're on the clifftop end. Our full area-by-area breakdown, including the parts of the island we'd steer you away from, is in [where to stay in Camiguin](/guides/where-to-stay-in-camiguin).

## A five-day honeymoon, hour by hour

Not a schedule. A shape. Adjust it to the weather and how late you want to sleep.

### Day 1 — Arrive and stop

Fly in from Cebu, about fifty-five minutes. The airport is fifteen minutes from the cliff and we collect you, so the day's logistics end at baggage claim.

Don't plan anything. Swim in the infinity pool as the afternoon cools. Then a **sunset kayak at the Sunken Cemetery** — paddling out to the white cross standing alone in the sea as the light goes gold and then rose. It's quiet, slightly haunting, and the right way to understand this island before you do anything else.

Dinner: tapas and paella by the water. *Gambas al ajillo* to start, because it should be.

### Day 2 — The sea, early

Up before light. A **private banca to White Island** at sunrise — the bare white sandbar in open water with the volcano catching first light behind it. We time the boat to the tide table and book it the night before, so you're the only ones there for a while. Everything about why sunrise matters is in our [White Island guide](/guides/white-island-camiguin).

On the way back, the **Cantaan Giant Clam Sanctuary** — snorkelling directly above giant clams the size of armchairs, in clear shallows.

Back by nine, salt in your hair, with the whole day still ahead. Breakfast by the sea — *Almusal sa Bahay*, our heritage table of *tsokolate*, *pan de sal con keso de bola*, garlic adobo and Camiguin mango. Then do nothing until the light softens.

Evening: **Tahimik Nights.** Camiguin has very little light pollution, and on a clear night the sky over the Bohol Sea is genuinely extraordinary. Oil lamps, no screens, cacao, and more stars than either of you has seen in years.

### Day 3 — The island, slowly

Take the ring road. A scooter if you both like that, an SUV with a driver if you'd rather watch.

**Katibawasan Falls** in the morning, before anyone else — seventy metres of water into a cold, clear pool. **Sto. Niño Cold Spring** through the heat. The **Gui-ob Church Ruins** and the **Sunken Cemetery** in the late afternoon, when the light comes sideways through the empty windows.

Finish at **Ardent Hot Springs** at dusk, when the air cools and volcano-warmed water finally makes sense.

### Day 4 — The slow day

The one people wish they'd built in.

**Sunrise Saludo** — gentle yoga on the cliff edge as the sun comes up over the sea, with Mantigue on the horizon. A **hilot** massage through the warm part of the day: traditional Filipino healing touch, local oils, volcanic salt foot soak, native herbal tea.

Late afternoon, **La Merienda** — a sunset picnic laid out on the cliff, or barefoot on White Island's sandbar if you'd rather. Ensaymada, churros con tsokolate, sangria or fresh coconut, woven mats, and the sky doing its slow work.

That's the whole day. That's the point.

### Day 5 — One more morning

Whatever you've decided is yours. A last swim at first light with the pool to yourselves. A **cooking session** in the kitchen if you'd like to take something home — *paella filipina*, *rellenong bangus*, learned properly and then eaten.

Or **Mantigue Island** — white sand, forest, and a coral drop-off with the best snorkelling in the region — if you want one more day on the water.

Then a slow breakfast, and the fifteen-minute drive back to a runway.

## The honest reasons it might not be your island

We'd genuinely rather you booked somewhere else than arrived disappointed.

**There is no nightlife.** None. A drink on a terrace and then bed. If part of your honeymoon picture involves a bar, a band or a beach club, Camiguin will feel empty rather than peaceful.

**The main beaches are black sand.** Camiguin is volcanic and its ring-road beaches are dark. The white sand is real and spectacular but it's *offshore*, at White Island and Mantigue, and you take a small boat to it. If walking out of your room onto white sand is the non-negotiable image in your head, be honest with yourself about that now.

**The restaurant scene is small.** There are good places, and resort kitchens do the heavy lifting. But you will not have a different celebrated restaurant every night. Most couples eat where they're staying, which is why choosing a property with a kitchen you actually want to eat in matters more here than elsewhere.

**Boat days depend on the sea.** In the greener months, keep one flexible day so the banca can be moved rather than cancelled.

**And it's small.** Five days is close to the natural length. Ten days here would be lovely for the right couple and long for most.

If you read those five and thought "that's exactly what I want" — this is your island.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Camiguin good for a honeymoon?
Very. It's quiet, safe, dramatic and small enough that nothing feels like effort — sunrise on an empty sandbar, volcano-warmed springs, cliff-edge glamping and sunsets over open sea. It suits couples who want privacy and slowness. It does not suit couples who want nightlife or a beach-club scene.

### How many days do you need in Camiguin for a honeymoon?
Five nights is the sweet spot: two for the island's highlights, one for the ring road, and two that belong entirely to you. Three works but leaves no room for a slow day, which is usually the day people remember most.

### What is the most romantic place to stay in Camiguin?
The clifftop end of Mambajao's north-west coast, where the land rises above the water and sunsets arrive over open sea. Look for sea-view rooms with private terraces, or cliff-edge glamping — a proper bed under canvas on a private deck above the Bohol Sea.

### When is the best time for a honeymoon in Camiguin?
May, June or September. All three are warm and calm with the island close to empty. March and April are the driest but busiest, late October is Lanzones Festival week and the loudest of the year, and November to January is the wettest.

### Is Camiguin better than Boracay or Palawan for a honeymoon?
For privacy, yes. Boracay and Palawan are more famous, more developed and considerably busier. Camiguin trades restaurants and nightlife for genuine quiet, shorter transfers and the feeling of having a place to yourselves — which for many honeymooners is the entire point.

### Does Camiguin have white-sand beaches?
Its main ring-road beaches are dark volcanic sand. The white sand is offshore and spectacular — White Island, a bare sandbar reached by a ten-minute boat ride, plus Mantigue Island and the Giant Clam Sanctuary shallows. If white sand outside your door is essential, factor that in.

### How much does a honeymoon in Camiguin cost?
At the comfortable end, roughly ₱6,000–10,000+ per person per day, plus flights of about ₱1,300–1,900 one way from Cebu. That covers a sea-view room, private boat trips, a driver, and dining out. Our full budget breakdown covers every line.

### Is there nightlife in Camiguin?
Essentially none, and that is deliberate. Evenings are a drink on a terrace, dinner by the water, and stars. Couples who want bars or clubs should look elsewhere; couples who want silence after dark will find it here.

### Can you arrange a proposal or anniversary surprise in Camiguin?
Yes — small resorts do this well because they're small. A private sunrise banca to the sandbar, a picnic set out on the cliff at golden hour, or a table apart with the sea below. Tell your hosts in advance rather than on the day.

### Is Camiguin safe for couples?
Very. It has very low crime and a relaxed, unguarded culture — people leave helmets on parked scooters without a thought. Visitors consistently mention the ease of it as one of the first things they notice.
