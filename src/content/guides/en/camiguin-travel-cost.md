# How Much Does a Camiguin Trip Cost? A Real Budget (2026)

Here is the short version, and it surprises people: **Camiguin is cheap once you're on it.** The island's entrance fees are measured in tens of pesos. A waterfall costs less than a coffee. A whole day of scooter freedom on a sixty-four-kilometre ring road costs less than a taxi ride in Manila.

What isn't cheap is **getting here**. On a three-day trip, the flight is usually the single largest line on your budget — bigger than all your activities put together, and often bigger than your room.

Understand that one thing and you plan a Camiguin trip properly: book the flight early and stop worrying about everything else.

Below is what it actually costs, in pesos, with the fees we send guests off with every week. All figures are indicative — Philippine fees move without much announcement, so treat them as planning numbers and confirm on the ground.

*Written by the family at Txaleta de Camiguin. Last updated August 2026.*

## The short answer: three daily budgets

Per person, per day, **excluding the flight in**:

| Style | Daily budget | What that looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Backpacker** | **₱1,200 – ₱1,800** | Hostel or basic room, shared scooter, carinderia meals, entrance fees, one boat trip |
| **Mid-range** | **₱3,000 – ₱4,500** | Guesthouse or mid-range resort, own scooter, restaurant meals, all the attractions |
| **Comfortable** | **₱6,000 – ₱10,000+** | Boutique or sea-view room, driver or SUV, private banca, dining out properly |

Add the **flight**, which is a one-off: roughly **₱1,300–1,900 one way** from Cebu, occasionally ₱1,219 on a seat sale, and past ₱3,000 at peak.

For a rough foreign-currency sense, the peso has historically traded somewhere around ₱50–60 to the US dollar — check the live rate, because that band moves.

## Getting there: your biggest single cost

Every route onto the island, with real door-to-door numbers. The full breakdown lives in [how to get to Camiguin from Cebu](/guides/how-to-get-to-camiguin-from-cebu).

| Route | Cost (PHP) | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Fly Cebu → Camiguin** (Cebgo, direct) | ~₱1,800 all-in incl. transfers | ~2 hr 20 min |
| **Fly Cebu → Cagayan de Oro + bus + RoRo ferry** | ~₱2,300 – 2,600 | ~5–6 hr |
| **All-ferry via Bohol** (fastcraft + bus + ferry) | ~₱1,720 | ~9–10.5 hr |
| Cebu city → Mactan airport (bus / taxi) | ₱50 / ~₱250 | ~1 hr |
| Camiguin airport → Mambajao (tricycle) | from ~₱50 | ~15 min |

**The counterintuitive bit:** the cheapest route is barely cheaper. The all-ferry Bohol chain saves you a few hundred pesos and costs you seven hours. Unless the journey itself is the point, fly.

**Where you actually save:** booking early. Camiguin's airport takes roughly one small turboprop a day from Cebu, so seat prices climb steeply as it fills. The difference between booking in January and booking in September for an October trip can be more than the rest of your week's activities combined.

## Accommodation

Nightly, for a double room. Rates rise sharply during Holy Week, the Filipino summer (late March–May), [Lanzones Festival week](/guides/camiguin-lanzones-festival) in October and Christmas–New Year.

| Type | Per night | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Hostel dorm / basic room | ₱800 – ₱1,500 | Fan or basic aircon, simple or shared bath |
| Guesthouse / inn | ₱1,500 – ₱2,800 | Private room, aircon, hot water, usually no pool |
| Mid-range beach resort | ₱2,800 – ₱5,000 | Pool, restaurant, beach access, tours arranged |
| Boutique / premium | ₱5,000 – ₱12,000+ | Sea views, design, breakfast, real service |
| Glamping / unusual stays | varies widely | A proper bed somewhere you'd never otherwise sleep |

Two things worth checking before you compare prices: **is breakfast included** (on this island it's often the best meal of the day and it's served over the sea), and **is the airport transfer included, or extra** — asking up front saves both the fare and the negotiation. We break the coasts and areas down in [where to stay in Camiguin](/guides/where-to-stay-in-camiguin).

## Getting around the island

| Option | Cost |
|---|---|
| Scooter | ₱450 / day |
| SUV / AUV | ₱2,850 / day (₱1,500 half day) |
| Local driver (added to a vehicle) | +₱1,000 / day (+₱500 half day) |
| Tricycle, short hop | from ~₱50 |
| Shared multicab / jeepney across the island | ~₱25 |
| Private multicab (up to 8 people) | ~₱1,700 |
| Jet ski | ₱7,000 / 3 hrs (+₱1,500 per extra hour) |

**There is no Grab or ride-hailing on Camiguin.** Budget for a scooter, a resort transfer or a tricycle — not an app.

For most couples, a scooter for two days (₱900 total) covers the entire island. That is the single best value on this list.

## Entrance fees: the cheap part

This is where Camiguin quietly rewards you. Every headline attraction costs less than a cocktail.

| Attraction | Fee per person |
|---|---|
| Katibawasan Falls | ~₱75 |
| Ardent Hot Springs | ~₱50 |
| Sto. Niño Cold Spring | ~₱50 |
| Bura Soda Water Pool | small, similar range |
| Mantigue Island environmental fee | ~₱75 |
| White Island environmental fee | ~₱50 – 75 |
| Sunken Cemetery / Gui-ob Ruins | small or free to view from shore |
| Walkway to the Old Volcano | small |

**Boat fares** are charged per boat, not per person, which makes them dramatically cheaper split between four:

| Boat trip | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| [White Island](/guides/white-island-camiguin) banca, round trip | ~₱550 per boat (usually up to 4–6 people) |
| Mantigue Island banca | typically ₱550 – 800 per boat, plus the env. fee |
| Cantaan Giant Clam Sanctuary | arranged with your boatman, small fee |

**Guided Mt. Hibok-Hibok ascent** and **birdwatching inside the protected forest** both require accredited guides and permits. Reported birding fees have run around ₱1,200 per group of three plus ₱200 per person environmental fee, but those figures come from an older write-up — confirm current rates with the tourism office, and don't budget from a blog.

Add it up: **a full day of the island's greatest hits — a waterfall, two springs, the ruins and the sunken cemetery — costs under ₱300 per person in fees.** The scooter to reach them costs more than the sights themselves.

## What people forget to budget for

1. **ATMs.** There are few on Camiguin and they run out of cash, especially at weekends and during festival week. Bring pesos with you from Cebu. This is the single most common practical mistake visitors make.
2. **The airport transfer.** Ask whether it's included before you compare room rates.
3. **The 5% service charge** on resort dining.
4. **Guide and permit fees** for Hibok-Hibok and protected-area birding — not large, but not zero, and easy to overlook.
5. **A weather buffer day.** Boat trips depend on the sea. If your whole trip is three days and one is a washout, you've lost a third of it. See [best time to visit Camiguin](/guides/best-time-to-visit-camiguin) for which months are calmest.
6. **Peak-date pricing.** Four windows push everything up: Holy Week, the Filipino summer, Lanzones Festival week and Christmas–New Year.

## Where to save, and where not to

**Save on:** transport around the island (a scooter beats a driver for two people), food (carinderias are genuinely good), and boats (fill the boat — the fare is per vessel, so four people pay a quarter each).

**Don't save on:** the flight, by booking late. Or on a room with a view, if the view is why you came — on this island, where you wake up is a large part of what you're buying, and the difference between a windowless room and one with the Bohol Sea at eye level is smaller than most people assume.

**And the free things are the best things:** sunset from any west-facing stretch of coast, the stars over the Bohol Sea on a clear night, the black-sand beaches at golden hour, and the ring road itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a trip to Camiguin cost?
Excluding flights, budget roughly ₱1,200–1,800 per person per day as a backpacker, ₱3,000–4,500 mid-range, and ₱6,000–10,000+ for a comfortable trip. Flights from Cebu run about ₱1,300–1,900 one way. A three-day mid-range trip for one person lands around ₱13,000 plus airfare.

### Is Camiguin expensive?
No — it is one of the better-value islands in the Philippines. Entrance fees to the main attractions are around ₱50–75 each, a scooter is ₱450 a day, and boat fares are charged per boat rather than per person. The flight in is usually the largest single cost of the trip.

### What are the entrance fees in Camiguin?
Roughly ₱75 for Katibawasan Falls, ₱50 for Ardent Hot Springs, ₱50 for Sto. Niño Cold Spring, and ₱75 environmental fee for Mantigue Island. White Island charges around ₱50–75 per person plus the boat. A full day of the island's main sights costs under ₱300 per person in fees.

### How much is the boat to White Island?
Around ₱550 for the banca round trip, typically covering up to four to six people, plus an environmental fee of roughly ₱50–75 per person. Because the boat is priced per vessel, splitting it between four makes it very cheap per head.

### How much is the flight from Cebu to Camiguin?
Typically ₱1,300–1,900 one way on Cebgo, dropping to around ₱1,219 on seat sales and rising past ₱3,000 at peak dates or for walk-up fares. There is roughly one flight a day on a small turboprop, so prices climb steeply as it fills — book early.

### How much money should I bring to Camiguin?
Bring cash. ATMs on the island are few and frequently out of money, particularly at weekends and during Lanzones Festival week. Withdraw what you need in Cebu or Cagayan de Oro before you arrive, and budget your daily spend plus a comfortable margin.

### Can you visit Camiguin on a budget?
Very easily. Basic rooms start around ₱800, carinderia meals are ₱80–150, entrance fees are tiny and a shared scooter is ₱450 a day. The main costs are the flight and, if you are travelling alone, the boat fares that are cheaper shared.

### How much does a scooter cost in Camiguin?
Around ₱450 per day, which covers the entire 64-kilometre ring road at your own pace. An SUV or AUV runs about ₱2,850 per day (₱1,500 half day), with a local driver a further ₱1,000 per day.

### Is food expensive in Camiguin?
No. A meal at a local carinderia runs ₱80–150, a casual restaurant main ₱250–450, and a resort restaurant main ₱400–800. Local beer is ₱70–120. Many resorts add a 5% service charge on dining.

### What is the cheapest way to get to Camiguin?
The all-ferry chain via Bohol — fastcraft from Cebu to Tagbilaran, bus to Jagna, then ferry to Balbagon — at roughly ₱1,720. But it takes 9 to 10.5 hours against about 2 hours 20 minutes to fly, and the flight is often only a few hundred pesos more.
